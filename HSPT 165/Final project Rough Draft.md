## Appetizers/Zensai

### Vinegar marinated octopus and seaweed
- Marinated in vinegar, with slight amount of sugar and soy sauce

### Sancerre
- Old world
- due to the high acidity of the vinegar from the food I recommend similarly high acid wine.
- To prevent ruining the texture and freshness of the seafood taste I also recommend a low tannin wine.
- Combining both of these options I believe Sancerre or other sauvignon blanc with high acid and low tannin to be a good pairing with this good.

### Reason I picked this pairing
- This seafood dish is my personal favorite and popular among traditional Japanese dishes. 
- It is also very refreshing for spring and warm/hot summer day, and a good starter for preparing the foundation for the later course meal. 
- The wine could also very well add to the simple dish with hints of citrus completing the meal and also Sancerre's mineral flavoring could add detail to the flavor of the seaweed. 

### FIT
Flavor
- Main competitor for the flavor of the wine is the large portion of the vinegar used in the dish.
- Both the dish and the wine is light weight and a good start to kick off the meal
Intensity
- Intensity is very adjustable for the marinate, I recommend adjusting the marinate's proportions based on the drink that is paired with the dish to not make the dish too intense to make the drink flavorless.
Texture
- By using the low tannin wine we can preserve the texture of the seaweed and fresh octopus. 
- Bringing a high tannin wine would make the dish metallic or bitter and ruin the start of the 5 course meal.

## Soup

### Osuimono
- Clear japanese soup
- While the ingredients can be anything, I recommend 
	- shiitake mushrooms and clams for slight earthy flavoring.
	- yuzu peel for citrus aromas similar to the drink.
	- white fish for texture

### Koshu
- New world
- Dry white from japan
- yuzu, jasmine, and white peach aromas
- Low alc, slightly high acidity to balance out with the soup
- Also shares similar after taste as sake. I assume this is due to the warm climate

### Reason I picked this pairing
- Clear soups are the most classic soups to exist in Japanese cuisine and while sake is usually paired with this dish, I wanted to see other alternative drinks that are paired with this dish.  
- I then found japanese produced wine called Koshu wine and based on its description of and citrus, lychee, and peach aromas, I thought it would be interesting pairing.
- Also based on the drink with its usual light alcohol and slight hint of yuzu and acidity would very well could balance the dish.

## Salad

Spinach Ohitashi (Horenso no Ohitashi)  
Blanched spinach soaked in dashi, usukuchi soy sauce, and mirin.  
Muscadet (Old world)  
Crisp, dry white with high acidity and minerality.

**Reason I picked this pairing**  
Ohitashi is a classic washoku side, light and umami-rich like goma-ae but simpler. Traditional in kaiseki, fits pure Japanese theme post-soup.

**FIT**
- **Flavor**: Dashi umami matches Muscadet's minerals; acidity cuts soy.
- **Intensity**: Both light-bodied starters.
- **Texture**: Crisp wine preserves tender spinach without overpowering.

## Entreè

Yakitori (grilled chicken skewers, shio yaki salt style)  
Chicken thigh skewers lightly salted, grilled over binchotan charcoal.  
Pinot Noir (New world, e.g. Oregon)  
Light red, fruity with low tannins, earthy notes.

**Reason I picked this pairing**  
Yakitori is iconic izakaya entree, traditional grilling method with minimal seasoning. Builds on kaiseki progression after lighter courses; wine adds depth vs sake.

**FIT**

- Flavor: Fruit complements chicken char; earthiness echoes smoke.
- Intensity: Medium matches grilled protein without overwhelming.
- Texture: Low tannins avoid drying meat.
