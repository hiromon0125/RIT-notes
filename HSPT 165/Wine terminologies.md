
## New world vs Old World

New world is very punchy and impactful. New world is preferred with food pair, but old world people can definitely just drink as is. 



>Terroir - the term used to describe the condition and biome around vineyards

Vineyard's conditions compared between New world and Old world:
1. Climate is not very different for either worlds:
	- Continental
	- Mediterranean
	- Maritime
2. Weather(varies from year to year)
	- Old world 
		- Tends to lean towards cooler European climates 
		- More variable weather with shorter seasons and risks frosts leading to slower grape ripening, higher acidity, lighter body, and lower alcohol levels.
	- New world
		- Warmer and sunnier; more humid conditions
		- i.e. California, Australia
		- Allows longer time to hand for riper grapes leading to fuller body, fruit-forward flavors, higher alcohol, and softer acidity
3. Soil
	- Old world
		- emphasis on poor fertility and mineral rich terroir 
		- limestone, chalk, slate, and marl soil types are common 
		- offers good drainage and low fertility that stress vines for concentrated flavors
		- rocky soils contribute to minerality and acidity
	- New world
		- varies widely but often support riper fruit through better nutrient retention
		- California offers gravel soils
		- New Zealand offers Volcanic soils
		- Australia offers terra rossa soils
		- South America offer clay-loam soils
		- Often richer and more fertile soils
		- Also good water retention and warmth for bolder, fruitier wines
		- Some places also tries mimic old world restraints, so New world just has more variations
4. Grape selection
	- Old world
		- focuses on regionally mandated varieties suited for local terroir
		- often blended
		- prioritizes terroir harmony(uniform) over variety
		- Burgundy use only Pinot Noir and Chardonnay
		- Bordeaux uses Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, and others
		- Mixing selections would introduce complexity in the flavor
	- New world
		- emphasis on single varietals labeled prominently with experimental plantings(more innovative)
		- Experimentative with selections via clones, international varieties, and single-varietal focus for fruit expression.
		- Napa uses Cabernet
		- Marlborough specializers in Sauvignon Blanc
5. Diseases, Fungi, and Pests
	- Europe was once devastated by imported vines from North America due to new mildews local to North America
	- Old world
		- Vines in Europe lacked resistance forcing vineyards to mass graft and use chemicals and pests
	- New world
		- Native American vines evolved resistance to local phylloxera, mildews, and nematodes allowing some ungrafted vines. 

Winemaking techniques compared between Old world and New world
1. Grape Quality
	- reflects environments and cultural differences
	- Old World
		- higher acidity, moderate sugar , and phenolic maturity on structure
		- complex, mineral, and earthy notes
	- New world
		- high sugar and alcohol
		- softer tannis
		- bold fruit flavors like berries or tropical notes
		- irrigation and canopy management and enhance consistency and ripeness
2. Fermentation
	- Differs due to tradition
	- Old world 
		- relies on wild yeasts and neutral vessels for terroir expression
		- indigenous yeasts drive spontaneous fermentation in large oak barrels, concrete eggs or amphorae at ambient temperatures
		- causes site specific flavors 
		- minimal interventions
	- New world
		- uses controlled commercial yeasts and precise temperature management for consistency and fruit preservation
		- commercial cultured yeasts ensure reliable starts
		- stainless steel tanks with temperature controls
		- temp is cooled for whites, warmed for reds
		- extended maceration or malolactic fermentation enhance fruit and body tailored to market preferences
3. To oak or unoak
	- differs tied to style goals
	- Old world
		- favors subtle or no oak to highlight the terroir
		- uses neutral french oaks or alternatives like concrete to add subtle spice and structure
		- unoaked style prevail in crisp whites or light reds
		- again likes to emphasize minerality and acidity over wooden flavors
	- New world
		- leans towards more prominent oak for added complexity and approachability
		- American oak barrels infuse vanilla, coconut, and toast
		- enhance fruit-forward wines like california chardonnay
		- Shorter aging in new, smaller, barrels boosts intensity, through trends toward less oak align with fresh unoaked preferences
4. Maturation
	- Differs due to tradition and drinkability goals
	- Old world
		- extended barrel aging for complexity
	- New world
		- Shorter to preserve ripe fruit with wines designed for drinking wthin 3-10 years
		- Napa Cabernet now rivals old world longevity through balanced acidity and tannins
5. Packaging, closure, and label
	- Old world
		- prioritize prestige with cork seals
		- packaging shapes differ like Bordeaux's straight sided or Burgundy's sloping shoulders for heritage
		- Closures remain dominant in premium old world wines for slow oxygenationand aging prestige, despite TCA risks 
		- Labels highlight appellation assuming consumer knowledge of blends and terroir
	- New world
		- emphasis on accessibility via varietal info and alternative closures
		- packaging opts for uniform, branded bottles prioritizing visibility and transport efficiency
		- Closures embraces screw caps for reliability freshness and cost
		- Labels lists grape varietals prominently for easy selection with bold graphics and winery branding

## Wine Body
Body is about how “heavy” or “thick” the wine feels on your palate. Like mouthfeel, alcohol, acidity, and residual sugar.
**Heavy** usually means punchy and has noticeable flavor and structure.
Sometimes this can be guessed based on alcohol levels like for white wine.

For red:
- light body: skim milk
- medium body: 2% milk? moderate weight to the mouth
- full body: is whole milk

For whites its usually just differentiated via alcohol level
- Light-bodied: Under 12.5% ABV, crisp and refreshing
- Medium-bodied: 12.5–13.5% ABV, balanced and smooth
- Full-bodied: Over 13.5% ABV, rich and creamy

## Chardonnay

Chardonnay is a green-skinned grape variety primarily used to produce white wine.
Started in Burgundy region of France but now grown world wide.

### Oaked and Unoaked
1. Oaked Chardonnay will develop buttery taste and converts to high acidic flavoring, adds vanilla, toast, baking spices, caramel, and nutty tones, creating a fuller body.
2. Unoaked Chardonnay will stay stay crisp and bright, highlighting green apple, citrus, pear, and mineral notes like chalk or flint without butter or wood influence
